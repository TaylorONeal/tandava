## Brief structure

Every brief uses the same sections so they stay comparable:

0. Verification limits (what could not be checked, and confidence levels)
1. The uncomfortable finding up front (is this even a competitor)
2. Company and positioning (with extracted positioning statement)
3. Pricing (current, with historical noted as stale)
4. Feature comparison — **two** matrices: designed scope, and verified capability
5. Strengths and weaknesses, both sides
6. Opportunities
7. Threats, including the nightmare scenario
8. Strategic implications: build / deprioritize / differentiate vs parity / do-not-say
9. What to monitor
10. Open questions

## Rules

- **Two matrices, always.** Scope comparison flatters us because Tandava has broad design work and
  thin verification. Publishing scope alone produces a comparison that does not survive a trial.
  Rate Tandava on what is verified end-to-end, per `docs/HOSTED_PRODUCT_REVIEW.md` release labels:
  demonstrated UI, backend foundation, verified end-to-end, planned.
- **Be honest about competitor strengths.** A brief where we win everything is useless.
- **Cite the source and the date.** Pricing and feature pages change quarterly. Mark confidence as
  High (vendor docs), Medium (multiple third-party reviews), or Low (inferred).
- **Note egress blocks.** Several vendor and review domains are blocked by the network egress proxy
  in cloud sessions. Say so in section 0 rather than quietly working around it.
- **The "so what" is the point.** Section 8 is the deliverable. Everything above it is evidence.
